We'll now formalize **XEC-003 — Entity Relationships**, the third foundational specification within XEC (XEI Entity Contract).

So far, we've defined how an entity is identified and how it is classified. The next question is\:

**How do entities relate to one another without confusing ownership, structure, dependency, and governance?**

This is especially important for XEI because human and AI agents will eventually create, modify, combine, and evolve entities across multiple No Origins products.

## 1\. First principle: an entity is not defined by its location alone

Consider XEI itself.

Its canonical identifier is `no.xei`, but that string doesn't tell us everything about its relationships.

For example\:

- No Origins establishes the namespace in which XEI is identified.
- XEI contains foundational domains such as XEC and XGA.
- XEI depends on certain contracts to operate consistently.
- XEI's evolution is governed by policies defined through XGA.

Those are four different relationships. They should not be treated as interchangeable simply because they involve the same entity.

The foundational rule I propose is\:

> An entity's identity remains distinct from its structural, functional, and governance relationships.

This permits the system to evolve without unnecessarily changing canonical identities.

## 2\. Define the initial relationship vocabulary

I propose eight relationship types, arranged by responsibility.

| Relationship | Meaning |
|--------|--------|
| `contains` | One entity structurally includes another |
| `belongs-to` | An entity is assigned to a parent or domain |
| `depends-on` | One entity requires another |
| `implements` | An entity realizes a contract or specification |
| `conforms-to` | An entity satisfies a defined contract |
| `references` | An entity points to another without depending on it |
| `owned-by` | An accountable owner is assigned |
| `governed-by` | An entity is subject to a governance authority or framework |

The first six are primarily **structural and semantic relationships** defined by XEC.

The last two connect XEC with XGA: XEC defines how these relationships are represented, while XGA determines their valid authority, permissions, and governance consequences.

An important distinction: `owned-by` means accountable ownership, not necessarily technical containment or namespace ownership.

## 4\. Define a universal relationship contract

Every relationship should have an explicit source, target, and kind.

Here's the minimal proposed format\:

```yaml

relationship:
  id: rel-001
  source: no.xei
  predicate: contains
  target: no.xei.xec
```

For relationships that need additional precision, we can attach metadata\:

```yaml

relationship:
  id: rel-002
  source: no.xei.xec
  predicate: governed-by
  target: no.xei.xga

  attributes:
    status: active
    effective_from: null
    evidence: []
```

The `rel-001` and `rel-002` identifiers here are illustrative local record IDs, not finalized canonical IDs.

I recommend that **ordinary relationships remain lightweight**\. They should become independently managed entities only if they need their own identity, lifecycle, accountability, or audit trail.

For example, a routine `references` relationship may need no more than three fields. A governance delegation may need an explicit authority, conditions, validity interval, and recorded approval.

## 5\. Establish relationship validation rules

We should not allow agents to create arbitrary relationships just because the syntax is valid.

A relationship must satisfy semantic constraints.

| Rule | Requirement |
|--------|--------|
| Reference integrity | Source and target must identify valid entities |
| Predicate validity | Relationship kind must be registered |
| Type compatibility | Source and target types must be permitted for that predicate |
| Cardinality | Relationships must respect allowed counts |
| Cycle control | Containment cannot form cycles |
| Authority | Governed relationship changes must satisfy XGA policies |

Two particular distinctions deserve attention.

**Containment versus dependency\:** A system can depend on another system without containing it. The dependency may even point outside XEI, while containment describes a structural hierarchy.

**Conformance versus implementation\:** An entity may conform to a specification without implementing the specification itself. A Button component conforms to an interaction contract; a validator implements a contract-evaluation mechanism.

## 6\. Relationship cardinality and inheritance

Different relationships need different constraints.

For the first draft, I recommend the following defaults\:

| Relationship | Default constraint |
|--------|--------|
| `belongs-to` | At most one primary structural parent |
| `depends-on` | Multiple dependencies allowed |
| `conforms-to` | Multiple applicable contracts allowed |
| `owned-by` | One accountable owner; multiple contributors allowed |
| `governed-by` | Multiple applicable governance scopes allowed |

A structural parent, accountable owner, and governance authority can therefore be three different entities.

For policy inheritance, we should distinguish an explicit relationship from an inherited or computed one. For example, an entity may inherit an XEI-wide policy without storing a separate `governed-by` edge to that individual policy.

That distinction keeps the registry from accumulating redundant relationships.

## 7\. Test XEC-003 against real examples

**Case A — Foundational architecture** (Fits)

```text
no.xei
  ├── contains → no.xei.xec
  └── contains → no.xei.xga

no.xei.xec
  └── governed-by → no.xei.xga
```

XEC and XGA remain peers even though their responsibilities are interdependent.

**Case B — Design component** (Fits)

```text
Button
  ├── belongs-to → Component Library
  ├── depends-on → Semantic Tokens
  └── conforms-to → Interaction Contract
```

The Button can inherit its release from the library while conforming to contracts maintained elsewhere.

**Case C — AI design agent** (Fits)

```text
AI Design Agent
  ├── belongs-to → Agent Registry
  ├── implements → Evaluation Interface
  └── governed-by → XGA
```

A capability to evaluate designs does not itself authorize the agent to approve or publish changes.

These examples validate the basic structure, but we have not yet proved that the relationship vocabulary handles every future entity category.

## 8\. A refinement I recommend before stabilization

There is one potential problem with `contains` and `belongs-to`\: they might become redundant if both are stored independently.

I recommend treating `belongs-to` as the inverse of a designated primary structural `contains` relationship, unless an entity explicitly needs a different membership relationship.

Similarly, `owned-by` and `governed-by` shouldn't automatically be interpreted as synonyms.

This gives us a more reliable model in which relationships can have explicit inverse semantics and integrity constraints.

It also means the XEI Entity Registry can operate more like a knowledge graph than a folder hierarchy.

## 9\. XEC specification progress

## My recommendation for what comes next

We now have three complementary layers\:

- **XEC-001\:** What constitutes a valid entity?
- **XEC-002\:** What kind of entity is it?
- **XEC-003\:** How does it relate to other entities?

The next natural specification should be **XEC-004 — Entity Lifecycle**, covering creation, registration, modification, deprecation, archival, and replacement, along with inherited versus independent releases and architectural generations.

XEC-004 would define lifecycle states and transition semantics. XGA would separately determine which agents are authorized to trigger those transitions and what approvals are required.

That is where our original ideas about agent-driven evolution, independent versioning, and architectural generations become a coherent operating model.

**Status\:** XEC-003 v0.1.0 is now drafted conceptually. I recommend retaining it as a working specification until its predicates, cardinalities, inverse relationships, and cross-domain governance references have passed formal validation.
